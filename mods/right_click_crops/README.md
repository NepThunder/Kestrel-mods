# Right-Click Crops

Right-click a mature plant to harvest it and keep your garden growing.

A behaviour port of [Right-Click Crops](https://github.com/AyanoKaito/horizon-right-click-crops) (MIT) by AyanoKaito, following its registry description and its harvesting guide. This is a fresh implementation against the Kestrel SDK (the `actions` capability, API 21): the harvest is the game's own dig — a synthesized attack click, so loot, tool and Fortune effects are the game's ordinary ones — and the replant is the planting item used on the soil, the click a player would make. The original is a native mod.

## What matches the original

- **Sixteen families, in the original's three groups.** *Harvest and replant:* wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, nether wart, cocoa, torchflower, pitcher crop. *Harvest the upper growth, keep the base:* sugar cane, cactus, bamboo, kelp, twisting vines — and weeping vines, which keep their top anchor instead. *Harvest the fruit, keep the stem:* pumpkin and melon (the fruit must have its matching attached stem beside it).
- **Maturity is each family's own maximum age** (wheat, carrots and potatoes at 7; beetroot and nether wart at 3; cocoa and the torchflower crop at 2; the pitcher crop at 4, clicked on either half for one harvest). Immature plants are left alone.
- **Replanting costs exactly one planting item** — wheat seeds for wheat, a carrot for carrots, a potato (never a poisonous one; the dig's ordinary loot decides what drops), beetroot seeds, nether wart, cocoa beans (the pod's support and facing are kept: the bean goes back on the same log face), torchflower seeds, a pitcher pod. **Generated loot pays first, then your inventory**: the port watches for the harvest's drops to land in your inventory, and pays from your hand, your hotbar or your offhand — planting items never need to be selected first, and if the payment came from another hotbar slot, your own slot is selected back afterwards. With no planting item to be had, the crop is harvested and not replanted; a seed is never created from nothing.
- **Retained parts cost nothing**: no planting item is consumed for the tall plants or the fruits.
- **Per-family settings**: each of the sixteen families can be switched off in Options > Mods > Right-Click Crops, and is on until it is.
- **The leave-alone rules**: bone meal in either hand fertilises instead; crouching with an item in either hand is your own item use; shears on a pumpkin carve it. Sweet berries and glow berries are never managed. Holding right-click handles successive plants one at a time, as the original does through repeated interactions.
- **A refused harvest replants nothing**: if the world does not let the dig happen (adventure restrictions, protection), the plant is still standing when the port looks again, and no seed is spent on it.

## What's different, and why

- **Payment cannot come from the bag.** The original pays from anywhere in the inventory. A Kestrel client mod can read the whole inventory but can only *choose* among hotbar slots and use what the offhand holds; moving a stack from the bag into the hand is the inventory-actions district's (B6) verb, which doesn't exist yet. A planting item that is only in the bag leaves the crop harvested and unreplanted, exactly as if it weren't there.
- **No survival-only gate.** The original acts only in survival. The client SDK shows a mod no game-mode read (it is only used inside the host, to gate `recipes.give`), so the port cannot apply the gate: in creative, a right-click on a mature plant harvests it too. In adventure, the server's refusal still protects the plant, as above.
- **Some checks read the world's blocks, and those reads are single-player only.** Whether a cane is growth or the retained base, whether a torchflower stands on farmland, whether a pumpkin has its stem: these read the blocks around the plant through `world.get-block`, which the SDK lends to client mods in single player (v1). Where the reads don't answer (a remote server), the tall plants and the torchflower wait, unharvested; the fruit's stem check simply cannot be made there and is skipped, so a stemless placed pumpkin on a server would be harvested where the original leaves it. The soil crops, cocoa and the pitcher crop need no such reads and work the same everywhere.
- **Replanting from the harvest is timing, honestly.** Drops are items in the world until they are picked up; the port waits a few ticks for them to reach the inventory before falling back to what was already there, but a drop that lands late or is never picked up cannot pay. The inventory path is the reliable one, as in the original.
- **The swing is checked a tick ahead.** The original hooks the interaction itself. Here the click context is observed (use held, crosshair on a mature plant), the attack is armed, and it is swung one tick later only if the aim still rests on that plant — an `actions.attack` click carries no target, and the port will not swing blind.
